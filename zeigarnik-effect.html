Zeigarnik Effect

Unfinished business stays in memory.

Bluma Zeigarnik found that interrupted, incomplete tasks are remembered far better than completed ones. Open loops pull people back — the engine behind progress bars, streaks, and every “complete your profile” nudge.

Origin

Bluma Zeigarnik was a Lithuanian-Soviet psychologist who studied under Kurt Lewin in Berlin. Legend says the idea began at a café: waiters recalled unpaid orders perfectly and forgot them the moment the bill was settled.

Her 1927 experiments confirmed it — participants interrupted mid-task recalled those tasks roughly twice as well as finished ones. She went on to co-found experimental pathopsychology in Moscow, and the open-loop effect that bears her name became a staple of product design.

The Effect

Interrupted or incomplete tasks are remembered better than completed ones. An open loop creates a low-grade tension that keeps pulling attention back until the loop closes — completion releases it, and the memory fades.

Coined by
Bluma Zeigarnik (1927)
Source
On Finished and Unfinished Tasks
In practice
Show what’s left open
When to Use
  • You are designing onboarding and deciding how many setup steps to show before the user reaches real work.
  • A profile completeness meter is proposed, and the team is arguing over which fields it should count.
  • Background agents run for minutes with no visible state, and users keep reopening the tab to check.
How to Use
  • Name the remaining steps explicitly in the stepper, since specific counts recruit the tension that vague encouragement wastes.
  • Keep onboarding checklists short, real, and finishable, and show completeness somewhere between 60 and 90 percent.
  • Design an unmistakable done-state for every loop you open, because the release is the reward users came for.

Show the Remaining Steps

A stepper that shows two of four steps done recruits the effect honestly: the user can see exactly what’s open and what closing it takes. Vague progress (“almost there!”) wastes the tension it creates.

Ethics 05 / 08

Don’t Weaponize the Loop

Streaks that punish a missed day, carts that nag for weeks, fake “1 step left” prompts — manufactured incompleteness burns the trust the effect depends on. If the loop wasn’t opened by the user, closing it isn’t their job.

Test
Who opened the loop?

Burns trust
Respect

Closure 06 / 08

Let Loops Actually Close

The effect has a second half: completion releases the tension, and that release is the reward. Clear done-states, satisfying confirmations, and empty inboxes give users the closure the open loop promised.

The Zeigarnik Effect in the Age of AI

Agents leave loops open on your behalf now — drafts, running tasks, half-finished jobs. Showing what’s unfinished is how trust survives the delegation.

In the Age of AI 07 / 08

The Draft Left Open

An assistant that leaves a visible draft — half an email, a paused edit — pulls the user back to finish the thought. Resumable state is the Zeigarnik effect working for the user instead of on them.

Pattern
Visible, resumable drafts
Effect
Users return to finish
In the Age of AI 08 / 08

Agents Keep Loops Open

A background agent is an open loop the user didn’t used to have. Show the running task, its progress, and its finish — an invisible loop generates anxiety, a visible one generates trust.
