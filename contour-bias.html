Contour Bias

Rounded relaxes. Sharp alerts.

Moshe Bar and Maital Neta showed in 2006 that people prefer curved objects over sharp ones — angular contours read as threat at a glance. Roundness puts users at ease; sharpness demands attention. Both are tools.

Origin

Moshe Bar and Maital Neta at Harvard flashed everyday objects, each in a curved and a sharp-cornered version, one at a time for a fraction of a second. Viewers consistently preferred the curved versions, even with no time to reason about them.

Their follow-up work pointed at the mechanism: sharp-angled objects produced more activation in the amygdala, the brain’s threat detector. The preference for curves is not taste — it is a fast, low-level safety judgment that fires before deliberate thought.

The Bias

At a glance, people judge curved contours as safe and approachable and sharp contours as potentially threatening. The response is fast, automatic, and strongest under time pressure — which is exactly how interfaces are seen.

Origin
Moshe Bar and Maital Neta (2006)
Source
“Humans Prefer Curved Visual Objects”
In practice
Round to reassure, sharpen to alert
When to Use
  • Your design system needs a single corner-radius token and the team is split between sharp, subtle, and fully rounded.
  • A destructive confirmation dialog tests as too easy to dismiss, and participants say the warning did not feel serious.
  • A brand refresh for a precision or premium tool must decide whether soft corners undercut the sense of rigor.
How to Use
  • Round the corners on buttons, cards, inputs, and avatars so a control reads as safe to press before its label is read.
  • Keep warning triangles, error badges, and hazard marks angular, because a soft-cornered alert whispers where a pointed one shouts.
  • Pick one curvature scale and enforce it as a token across every button, dialog, and card instead of mixing radii per component.

The Split-Second Preference

Watches, sofas, letterforms — shown curved or angular for 84 milliseconds. Curved won, category after category. The preference held even when viewers could not say why, which is the signature of a pre-attentive judgment.

Boundary 05 / 08

A Bias, Not a Rule

Context can outweigh contour. Precision tools, luxury brands, and editorial design use sharp geometry to signal rigor and edge — and roundness everywhere can read as soft or childish. Choose the feeling first, then the corner.

Contour Bias in the Age of AI

Generated interfaces ship the same friendly radius by default, and AI products round themselves to feel harmless. Curvature has become a rhetoric — use it honestly.

In the Age of AI 07 / 08

The Radius-12 Default

Ask a model for a UI and you get the same gentle corners as everyone else — curvature chosen by statistics, not intent. When every product is equally rounded, contour stops carrying brand. Set your radius on purpose.

In the Age of AI 08 / 08

Rounded to Disarm

Assistants, robots, and AI mascots are styled soft on purpose — orbs, blobs, gentle arcs — because curves lower guard. That is fine when the system is benign, and manipulative when roundness is hiding what a system does.
