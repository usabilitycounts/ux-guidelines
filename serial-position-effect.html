Serial Position Effect

First and last stick. The middle fades.

Hermann Ebbinghaus found that people best recall the first and last items in a series — primacy and recency — while the middle blurs. The effect decides what goes at the ends of your menus, lists, and flows.

Origin

Hermann Ebbinghaus, the German psychologist who founded the experimental study of memory, documented the serial position effect in the 1880s — memorizing thousands of nonsense syllables on himself and plotting where recall failed.

His curves showed recall highest at the start of a list (primacy) and at the end (recency), with a sag in the middle; the explanations — early items rehearsed into long-term memory, late items still in working memory — came from 1960s research. The same experiments produced the forgetting curve and the spacing effect.

The Effect

When people recall a series, performance forms a U: the first items benefit from rehearsal into long-term memory, the last from still being in working memory, and the middle items lose both advantages. In interfaces, position is a memory decision.

Coined by
Hermann Ebbinghaus (1885)
Source
Memory: A Contribution to Experimental Psychology
In practice
Ends carry the weight
When to Use
  • A tab bar is being reordered and the team must decide which destination earns the first slot.
  • You are sequencing an onboarding flow that mixes a value story with permission prompts and account forms.
  • A long system prompt keeps having its middle instructions ignored during evaluation runs of an AI feature.
How to Use
  • Rank menu items by importance, then anchor the top two at the first and last positions of the list.
  • Park low-stakes content such as settings sub-pages, legal links, and edge cases in the middle of a list.
  • Open a flow with the value and close it with the win, keeping forms and permissions between them.

First Impressions Get Rehearsed

The first items in any sequence get the most attention and the most rehearsal — users return to them mentally while processing the rest. The first menu item, first onboarding screen, and first bullet do disproportionate work.

The curve 03 / 08

The U-Shaped Recall Curve

Plot recall against position and the shape is a U — high at both ends, sagging in the middle. Anything that must be remembered from the middle of a sequence needs extra help: emphasis, repetition, or relocation.

Serial Position in the Age of AI

Models have their own middle problem — long contexts recall their start and end best. The old memory curve found a new machine.

In the Age of AI 07 / 08

Prompts Have Ends Too

Instructions at the start and end of a long prompt are followed more reliably than ones buried in the middle. The same primacy-recency shape that governs human lists governs what a model attends to.

In the Age of AI 08 / 08

Lost in the Middle

Long-context models recall the start and end of their context best — retrieval quality sags for material in between. Feed a model a hundred documents and the middle ones need the most deliberate surfacing.
